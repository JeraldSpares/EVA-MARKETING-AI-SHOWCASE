# Enderun Colleges — Brand Voice & Messaging Guide
**For use by:** All agents
**Authority:** Marketing Director

---

## The Enderun Brand Personality

If Enderun were a person, they would be:
- A **Filipino professional** who studied in Switzerland and came back to give something back to the country
- **Confident but never arrogant** — they know they're good, but they don't need to shout it
- **Warm and genuinely caring** — they remember your name and ask about your family
- **Ambitious and forward-thinking** — always looking at what's next for their students and the industry
- **Proud of their roots** — Filipino at heart, world-class in execution

---

## Voice Dimensions

### 1. Aspirational (not elitist)
Enderun speaks to who students can become — not who they currently are. We lift, we don't exclude.

**Do:** "Your world-class career starts here."
**Don't:** "For the select few who deserve the best."

---

### 2. Warm (not corporate)
Our communications feel like they come from a person, not a committee.

**Do:** "We're rooting for you, [Name]. Don't miss this deadline."
**Don't:** "Applications must be submitted no later than the prescribed deadline."

---

### 3. Confident (not boastful)
We state facts with pride. We let results speak — without overselling.

**Do:** "Our graduates work at Mandarin Oriental, Four Seasons, and Shangri-La worldwide."
**Don't:** "We are the BEST hospitality school in all of Asia!!"

---

### 4. Filipino-Proud (not generic global)
We are specifically proud of what Filipino talent looks like on the world stage.

**Do:** "Proving that world-class Filipino hospitality professionals are born right here in BGC."
**Don't:** Generic international imagery or copy that erases our Philippine identity.

---

## Writing Rules by Content Type

### Headlines
- Max 8 words
- Use active verbs: Launch, Discover, Meet, Join, Build, Create
- Examples:
  - "Train Like a Swiss Hotel Professional"
  - "Where Filipino Excellence Meets Global Standards"
  - "Your BGC Classroom Awaits"

### Body Copy
- Short sentences (max 20 words each)
- One idea per paragraph
- Lead with benefit, not feature
- Feature: "We have Bloomberg terminals"
- Benefit: "Practice real-world financial analysis like the professionals you'll become"

### Social Captions
- Facebook: Can be longer (200–400 words), storytelling format OK
- Instagram: 100–150 words, emoji OK (2–3 max), punchy
- LinkedIn: 150–300 words, professional, data/insight-led
- TikTok: Caption is almost irrelevant — hook in the video itself

### Email Subject Lines
- Personal feel: Use [First Name] when possible
- Under 50 characters
- Avoid: ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation, spam trigger words ("FREE!!!", "URGENT")
- Good examples:
  - "A message for you, [First Name]"
  - "The Swiss connection that changes everything"
  - "Your application is waiting, [First Name]"

## Words to Use vs. Avoid

### Use These
| Word/Phrase | Reason |
|---|---|
| World-class | Core brand promise |
| Global | Aspirational, accurate |
| Les Roches | Unique differentiator — use often |
| Community | Warm, human feel |
| Industry-ready | Outcome-focused |
| BGC / McKinley Hill | Specific, premium location |
| Filipino excellence | Pride + specificity |
| Personalized | Speaks to small class size benefit |
| Immersion | Differentiator vs. theory-only schools |
| Career | Outcome-focused |

### Avoid These
| Word/Phrase | Reason |
|---|---|
| Cheap / affordable | Undermines premium positioning |
| Elite / exclusive | Elitist, exclusionary tone |
| Best school ever / #1 in Asia | Unsubstantiated, overselling |
| ASAP | Too casual/corporate-sounding |
| Leverage / synergize / utilize | Corporate jargon |
| Simply / just (minimizing) | Undersells what we offer |
| World-famous (overused, hard to verify) | Better: "globally recognized" |

## Taglish Guidelines (Filipino-English Mix)

Taglish is allowed and encouraged for:
- Facebook posts targeting parents and general community
- TikTok and Instagram Stories
- SMS messages
- Email subject lines (selective)

Taglish is NOT appropriate for:
- Press releases
- LinkedIn posts
- Formal program descriptions
- Academic or institutional documents

### Good Taglish Examples
- "Para sa mga nag-aaplika pa lang — ready na ba kayo?"
- "Ito na ang simula ng inyong world-class journey."
- "Mag-apply na — deadline na bukas!"
- "Kung pangarap mong magtrabaho sa pinakamagandang hotel sa mundo, dito ka nagsisimula."

## Tone Calibration by Season

| Season | Tone Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Peak Enrollment (Jan–Mar) | +30% urgency, +20% warmth, clear CTAs |
| School Year Start (June) | +40% celebration, community, excitement |
| Mid-Year Brand Building | Balanced — thought leadership + lifestyle |
| Foundation Day (Aug) | Pride, gratitude, institutional warmth |
| Year-End (Dec) | Reflection, gratitude, hope for what's next |

## Sample Copy: Before & After

**Before (generic, weak):**
> "Enderun Colleges is a school in BGC that offers various programs in hospitality, business, and culinary arts. We have many facilities and experienced faculty. Apply now."

**After (on-brand):**
> "In the heart of BGC, Enderun students train in commercial-grade kitchen labs, simulate real hotel operations, and access Bloomberg terminals — with professors who've actually worked the floors of the world's best hotels.
> This isn't just school. This is your career, starting now.
> Applications for SY 2026–2027 are open. [Apply here]"
